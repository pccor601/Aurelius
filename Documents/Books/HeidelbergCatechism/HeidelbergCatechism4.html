Heidelberg Catechism: A Guided Tutorial

Lord's Day 7: Questions 20-23

Question 20: Are all men, then, saved by Christ just as they perished through Adam?
Answer: No. Only those are saved who by a true faith are grafted into Christ and accept all his benefits(1).

1. Mt 7:14; Jn 1:12; 3:16, 18, 36; Romans 5:12-21; Rom 11:16-21.

We could get the impression that Romans 5:18 teaches universal salvation; however, even a cursary reading of the New Testament soon debunks that idea. Instead, Paul constructs a parallelism here: in the case of Adam, condemnation because of descent, and in the case of Christ, justification because of ingrafting. And not all are ingrafted into Christ. This ingrafting is by "a true faith" which means that it is not something we see with the eye of flesh, but we accept it because of the word of God. This includes the acceptance of all the benefits of Christ such as adoption, sanctification and eventual glorification.

Question 21: What is true faith?
Answer: True faith is a sure knowledge whereby I accept as true all that God has revealed to us in his Word(1). At the same time it is a firm confidence(2) that not only to others, but also to me(3) God has granted forgiveness of sins, everlasting righteousness, and salvation(4) out of mere grace, only for the sake of Christ’s merits(5). This faith the Holy Spirit works in my heart by the gospel(6).

1. Jn 17:3, 17; Heb 11:1-3; Jas 2:19.
2. Rom 4:18-21; 5:1; 10:10; Heb 4:16.
3. Gal 2:20.
4. Rom 1:17; Heb 10:10.
5. Rom 3:20-26; Gal 2:16; Eph 2:8-10.
6. Acts 16:14; Rom 1:16; 10:17; 1 Cor 1:21.

We notice in the answer to question 21 that our assurance is tightly bound to a certain conviction of the divine origin of scripture. We know that we are saved by faith in Christ because the bible tells us so. It's that simple. This turns faith into a sure knowledge. So where do we get that kind of faith. We cannot manufacture it ourselves, and Ephesians 2:8-10 tells us that it is entirely supernatural. See below:

8 For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, 9 not a result of works, so that no one may boast. 10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them.

The antecedent of "this" is "faith" which makes faith the gift of God. It is given by the Holy Spirit during the conversion process and is the indellible mark of true conversion. We NEVER have to convince a true Christian of the divine origin of the bible and if you catch yourself doing so you need to switch to evangelism because that is what they need.

Faith in Christ alone for salvation is the heart and soul of Protestant Evangelical Christianity and is what distinguishes it from all forms of false Christianity, especially the cults and Roman Catholicism. Because salvation is by faith alone it rests upon the foundation of grace alone which is the burden of Paul's teaching throughout much of the book of Romans.

Question 22: What, then, must a Christian believe?
Answer: All that is promised us in the gospel(1) which the articles of our catholic and undoubted Christian faith teach us in a summary.

1. Mt 28:19; Jn 20:30, 31.

Although there are many creeds that seek to summarise the gospel, the authors of the Heidelberg Catechism chose to use the Apostle's Creed which was written somewhere between the second and fourth centuries A.D.

Lord's Day 8: Questions 24, 25

Question 24: How are these articles divided?
Answer: Into three parts: the first is about God the Father and our creation; the second about God the Son and our redemption; the third about God the Holy Spirit and our sanctification.

Question 25: Since there is only one God(1) why do you speak of three persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit?
Answer: Because God has so revealed himself in his Word(2) that these three distinct persons are the one, true, eternal God.

1. Deut 6:4; Is 44:6; 45:5; 1 Cor 8:4, 6.
2. Gen 1:2, 3; Is 61:1; 63:8-10; Mt 3:16, 17; 28:18, 19; Lk 4:18; Jn 14:26; 15:26; 2 Cor 13:14; Gal 4:6; Tit 3:5, 6.

The doctrine of the Trinity is not a contradiction although it might sound like one. If the doctrine were to assert that God is both one person and three persons at the same time and in the same sense, that would be a contradiction. Or, if the doctrine were to assert that God were both one God and three Gods at the same time and in the same sense that too would be a contradiction. However, the doctrine asserts that God is both one God and three persons at the same time and in the same sense. So this does not turn one into three or three into one. The mystery of the doctrine is that the one God can subsist in three distinct persons. There is nothing in temporal human experience that exemplifies this type of being. In regard to human beings, there can be only one person for each one human being. A split personality does not violate this principle since such a person cannot have more than one personality at a time.

Failure to relate the trinity to temporal experience does not reflect a lack of understanding of the doctrine. If the trinity can be accurately defined then it can be said to be understood. The following are definitive statements on the trinity:
1. There is only one God. (Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 45:5).
2. The Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are each fully God (John 1:1; Acts 5:3–4; Colossians 2:9).
3. The Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are three distinct persons (Matthew 3:16–17).

Apostles Creed

1. I believe in God the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.
2. I believe in Jesus Christ, his only-begotten Son, our Lord;
3. he was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the virgin Mary;
4. suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, dead, and buried; he descended into hell.
5. On the third day he arose from the dead;
6. he ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of God the Father almighty;
7. from there he will come to judge the living and the dead.
8. I believe in the Holy Spirit;
9. I believe a holy catholic Christian church, the communion of saints;
10. the forgiveness of sins;
11. the resurrection of the body;
12. and the life everlasting.
